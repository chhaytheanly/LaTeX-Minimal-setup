    Accurate, real-time counting of small metallic fasteners on high-speed industrial conveyor belts remains a persistent bottleneck in manufacturing logistics. Manual visual inspection is cognitively demanding, error-prone, and economically unsustainable, while conventional weight-based estimation lacks the granularity required for modern supply-chain traceability. Existing automated solutions are further hampered by specular glare on reflective surfaces, dense part overlap, and the prohibitive computational cost of high-accuracy detectors challenges that collectively preclude reliable, always-on deployment at the factory edge.\\

    This thesis presents an end-to-end, cloud-free edge-AI framework for the real-time detection, classification, and tallying of metallic fasteners on industrial conveyors. At the core of the system lies YOLO11-GED, a lightweight object-detection architecture enhanced with a novel EMAc2f module that synergises three complementary mechanisms: Efficient Multi-Attention for suppressing specular artefacts, GhostNet-based feature reuse for parameter-efficient small-object representation, and Dynamic Upsampling for preserving spatial fidelity at reduced computational cost. To establish a rigorous baseline, nine detector configurations spanning three YOLO generations (YOLOv8n/s/m, YOLOv11n/s/m, YOLOv26n/s/m) are benchmarked on a purpose-built dataset of fastener images captured under realistic conveyor conditions, including variable illumination, motion blur, and dense clustering.\\

    The trained model is deployed on an NVIDIA Jetson embedded platform with TensorRT acceleration and interfaced with an Intel RealSense depth camera, achieving a target of mAP@0.5 ≥ 0.95 at ≥ 30 FPS with zero cloud dependency. Surrounding the vision engine, a full-stack production platform is implemented, comprising a Python inference service, an Express.js REST API, a persistent database for audit logs, a Redis layer for real-time telemetry, and dual operator interfaces (Web dashboard and Desktop application). The integrated system is validated on a physical conveyor test bench, demonstrating sustained, low-latency counting that eliminates manual recounts and reduces human error.\\

    Beyond the industrial deliverable, the work contributes a reproducible design-to-deployment methodology, quantitative ablation studies isolating the effect of each EMAc2f component, and documented evidence that tailored architectural modifications can push small-object recall under strict edge-compute budgets without sacrificing throughput.
